A Paired Noise Benchmark and Augmentation Study for Romanized Tamil}

\begin{abstract}
Tamil speakers routinely type Tamil in Latin letters (``Tanglish'') and software must convert it back to Tamil script, yet published transliteration models are trained and tested on carefully romanized text.
We measure what happens under informal typing for word-level Latin-to-Tamil transliteration with character-level sequence-to-sequence models.
We release a seeded, paired benchmark of seven Tamil-specific noise operators at four severities (28 noisy copies of the Dakshina test lexicon, each item tied to its clean form and references) and train 110 models: a BiLSTM with attention and a pre-LN Transformer at about 1M, 3M and 8M parameters with five seeds each, and eight training conditions with five seeds each for both architectures.
Our implementation reproduces the published Dakshina development CER of 8.4.
Vowel dropping and keyboard slips are the damaging operators: at $p{=}0.3$ they raise test CER from about 9 to 22--24, while vowel-length, doubled-consonant and letter-stretching noise cost only 1--2 points.
Training with Tamil-phonological noise cuts vowel-drop CER from 24.0 to 17.7 for the BiLSTM, but does not transfer to an operator left out of training (25.0), and costs about 0.8 clean CER.
Adding sampled back-transliterations (A4) gives the lowest mixed-noise CER (21.8 against 25.5) at no clean cost, although the clean gain of back-transliteration alone is explained by extra words, not by spelling variety.
At matched size the two architectures tie on clean text, and the Transformer loses 0.3--1.3 CER less under heavy mixed noise.
Fine-tuning the best model on 2.4M pairs with all six noise types gives one 12.6\,MB model that cuts mixed-noise CER from 21.8 to 17.8 at unchanged clean accuracy; real chat typing is only checked preliminarily.
\end{abstract}
