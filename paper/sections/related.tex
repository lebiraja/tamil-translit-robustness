\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Datasets and systems.}
Dakshina provides Tamil romanization lexicons and romanized Wikipedia sentences for twelve South Asian languages~\cite{roark2020dakshina}.
Aksharantar and the IndicXlit model extend coverage to 21 languages with 26 million pairs, mostly mined from monolingual and parallel corpora, and report a rise in Tamil top-1 accuracy from 56.0 to 67.7 when mined data are added~\cite{madhani2023aksharantar}.
Earlier systems include rule and statistical models such as Brahmi-Net~\cite{kunchukuttan2015brahminet}, multilingual transliteration~\cite{kunchukuttan2018orthographic,kunchukuttan2021largescale} and the NEWS shared tasks whose metrics we follow~\cite{chen2018news}.
Context-aware models, ensembles and byte-level pretrained models give further gains on sentences~\cite{kirov2024context,xue2022byt5}, and large language models are competitive on clean Tamil~\cite{azam2025beyond}.
Production keyboards use small finite-state models under tight latency budgets~\cite{hellsten2017keyboard}.

\textbf{Informal and noisy romanization.}
Wolf-Sonkin et al.\ describe variation in Latin-script input across South Asian languages, including optional aspiration and vowel doubling~\cite{wolfsonkin2019latin}.
For Tamil, a shorthand-typing study derives rules from a survey of 215 users but evaluates no neural model~\cite{herath2024tamzhi}; a shared task on ad-hoc romanization shows that all systems degrade~\cite{sumanathilaka2025indonlp}; and for Malayalam a BiLSTM loses about 15 CER points on vowel-omitted input~\cite{baiju2024malayalam}.
Code-mixed Tamil corpora such as TamilMixSentiment contain real informal text but have no Tamil-script references~\cite{chakravarthi2020tamilmix}.
Lexicon snapping and language-model reranking can recover accuracy on noisy input~\cite{mandal2018normalization,gulcehre2015monolingual}.

\textbf{Noise robustness and augmentation.}
Character models are brittle to noise they were not trained on, and robustness to one noise type transfers poorly to others~\cite{belinkov2018synthetic}.
A balanced mixture of deletions, insertions, substitutions and swaps improves robustness to typos at small clean cost but does not help stylistic variation~\cite{karpukhin2019training}.
For romanized language identification, synthetic data that reproduces human spelling variation beats larger models, whereas machine-romanized data overstate accuracy~\cite{benton2025romanizedlid,madhani2023bhasha,gharami2025indotranslit}.
Back-translation is the standard way of creating synthetic parallel data~\cite{sennrich2016backtranslation}, and we use its transliteration analogue.
Byte-level models are intrinsically more robust to character noise than subword models~\cite{xue2022byt5,sennrich2016bpe}.

\textbf{Architecture comparisons.}
Attention encoder-decoders~\cite{sutskever2014sequence,bahdanau2015neural,luong2015effective} and Transformers~\cite{vaswani2017attention} have been compared on character transduction with mixed outcomes~\cite{roark2020dakshina,kirov2024context,wu2021applying,moran2020effective}.
Moran and Lignos show that parameter count and weak statistics can reverse conclusions~\cite{moran2020effective}; we therefore match size, tuning budget and seeds.
Our work differs from all of the above in studying Tamil word transliteration under controlled, paired, severity-graded noise, with training remedies and architectures compared on the same footing.
