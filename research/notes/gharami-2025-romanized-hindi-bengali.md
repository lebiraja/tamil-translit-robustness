# Gharami et al. 2025 — Modeling Romanized Hindi and Bengali: Dataset Creation and Multilingual LLM Integration

**Citation.** Kanchon Gharami, Quazi Sarwar Muhtaseem, Deepti Gupta, Lavanya Elluri, Shafika Showkat Moni. 2025. "Modeling Romanized Hindi and Bengali: Dataset Creation and Multilingual LLM Integration." arXiv:2511.22769 (Nov 2025, preprint). Read in full (tables partially garbled in extraction).

## Content
**IndoTranslit**: ~1.8M Hindi + ~975k Bengali sentence pairs. Hindi: 1.66M pairs by IAST-based rule romanization (indic-trans) of IITB Hindi; **+150k "casual" romanizations generated by prompting Gemini-2.0 Flash Lite** "to simulate how Hindi speakers typically write Romanized text in casual settings", introducing vowel omission, consonant simplification, non-standard spelling; +10k Dakshina pairs. Bengali: rule-based heuristic romanizer over Wikipedia. Model: ~60M-param Marian Transformer, 32k shared SentencePiece vocab, RTX 4090. Results (Table IX): Hindi BLEU 77.57, CER 0.09, WER 0.13; Bengali BLEU 77.82; joint 73.15.

## Weaknesses (my assessment)
Training and test drawn from the same synthetic pipelines (rule-based / LLM-generated), so reported scores largely measure fit to its own generator; no evaluation on independent human romanizations; subword vocab for a character task; no Tamil.

## Useful second-hand pointers (from its related work; not verified by me)
- Baruah et al. (2024): Assamese social-media romanization study comparing PBSMT, BiLSTM+attention and Transformer — **BiLSTM reported best**.
- IndiDataMiner (IndoNLP 2025) LLaMA-3.1 Hindi system described as "robust to vowel omission".

## Relevance to us
- Shows the community moving to **LLM-generated informal romanization** as augmentation — a contrasting baseline to our rule/phonology-driven or sampling-driven noise. Also a cautionary example of evaluating on in-distribution synthetic data (cf. Bhasha-Abhijnaanam's 96% synthetic vs 80% human gap). Our evaluation must include human-produced noisy input.
